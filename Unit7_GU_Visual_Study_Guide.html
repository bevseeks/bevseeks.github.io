AGN vs. nephrotic syndrome

The single most-tested distinction in this unit — anchor on blood pressure and albumin.
FeatureAcute glomerulonephritisNephrotic syndrome
TriggerPost-strep, symptoms weeks laterIdiopathic — minimal change disease
UrineGross hematuria — tea / cola coloredMassive proteinuria; hematuria usually microscopic
Blood pressureHypertensiveUsually normal or low
AlbuminRoughly normalLow — drives the edema
CholesterolNormalHigh
Edema mechanismFluid retention from ↓ filtrationFluid shift from ↓ oncotic pressure

Renal diet — foods by electrolyte

The same lists used across AGN, nephrotic syndrome, AKI, CKD, dialysis, and HUS recovery — organized by which electrolyte drives the restriction, so you can apply it to whichever condition's labs are abnormal.
Sodium
Restricted with edema, hypertension, or fluid overload — AGN, nephrotic syndrome, CKD, dialysis
Lower-sodium choices
  • Fresh fruits and vegetables
  • Fresh meat, poultry, and fish (unprocessed)
  • Home-cooked meals seasoned with herbs/spices instead of salt
  • Unsalted crackers, bread, rice, and pasta
  • Low-sodium or no-salt-added canned goods
High-sodium — limit/avoid
  • Table salt, soy sauce, bouillon, seasoning packets
  • Canned soups and regular canned vegetables
  • Cured/deli meats, bacon, sausage
  • Cheese, pickles, olives, condiments (ketchup, mustard)
  • Fast food, frozen dinners, salty snacks (chips, pretzels)
Potassium
Restricted with oliguria/hyperkalemia — AKI, CKD, HUS recovery, between dialysis sessions
Lower-potassium choices
  • Apples, berries, grapes, pineapple
  • Green beans, cabbage, cauliflower, cucumber, peppers
  • White rice, white bread, white pasta
  • Peeled, boiled/leached potatoes (double-cooking lowers potassium)
High-potassium — limit/avoid
  • Bananas, oranges/orange juice, melons (cantaloupe, honeydew)
  • Potatoes (with skin), tomatoes/tomato sauce, spinach, avocado
  • Dried fruit (raisins, apricots, prunes)
  • Beans, lentils, and other legumes
  • Salt substitutes — often potassium chloride-based
Phosphorus
Restricted with hyperphosphatemia/hypocalcemia — AKI, CKD, dialysis; controlled by limiting protein and dairy
Lower-phosphorus choices
  • Fresh fruits and most fresh vegetables
  • White bread, white rice, corn or rice cereals
  • Egg whites
  • Light-colored sodas/lemon-lime soda (in moderation, phosphate-additive-free)
High-phosphorus — limit/avoid
  • Milk, cheese, yogurt, and other dairy
  • Dark colas — contain phosphoric acid additives
  • Processed and deli meats, organ meats
  • Nuts, seeds, and peanut butter
  • Whole grains/bran, beans, and chocolate
Fluid
Restricted with oliguria/fluid overload — AGN (acute), AKI, CKD, hemodialysis between sessions
Lower fluid-volume foods
  • Crackers, bread, and other dry foods
  • Meat, cheese, and other solid foods
  • Small, measured sips rationed across the day rather than large volumes at once
Counts toward fluid limit — track/limit
  • Soups and broths
  • Gelatin, ice cream, popsicles, and ice chips
  • Watermelon and other high-water-content fruit
  • Gravies and sauces
These lists are general renal-nutrition guidelines, not individualized prescriptions — the specific electrolytes restricted, and by how much, depend on the child's current labs, stage of disease, and dietitian plan. Protein is a related but separate consideration: it's kept at (not below) the RDA for age in CKD, and is NOT restricted in nephrotic syndrome.

Enuresis medication safety

DDAVP — first-line, works immediately. Watch dry mouth, headache, nausea.

Tofranil (imipramine) — second-line ONLY. TCA risk: cardiac arrhythmia, hypotension, hepatotoxicity.

Hypospadias / epispadias safety

No circumcision before repair — foreskin may be needed for reconstruction.

No tub baths until stent / catheter is removed.

Oxybutynin → constipation risk → prevent with fluids / stool softener.

AKI electrolyte chain

↓ H⁺/bicarb secretion → metabolic acidosis → worse hyperkalemia → Kussmaul respirations compensate → ↓ phosphate excretion → hyperphosphatemia → hypocalcemia → tremors / seizures.
