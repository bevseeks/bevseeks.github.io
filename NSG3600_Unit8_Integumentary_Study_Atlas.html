    Skin Infections, Inflammations & Infestations

    🔥 Burns — Priority Topic for USLO 3

    ⚠ Why burns are worse in children
    • Thinner skin → lower temp/shorter exposure causes a MORE severe burn than in an adult
    • Higher mortality with severe burns vs. older children/adults with comparable injury
    • Harder to communicate pain accurately
    • ↑ risk of fluid/heat loss, dehydration, metabolic acidosis
    • Higher body-fluid-to-body-mass ratio → greater cardiovascular risk
    • Immature immune system → ↑ infection risk
    • Less muscle mass/body fat → protein-calorie deficiency risk
    • Scarring is MORE severe than in adults; growth delay may follow a significant burn

    Depth of Burn Injury — with Depth-Specific Interventions

    DepthKey FeaturesSpecific Interventions
    Superficial (1st°)Erythema + pain 2–3 days (sunburn). Intact epidermis, no blisters. Peels, heals WITHOUT scarring.Cool compresses, aloe/moisturizer, oral NSAIDs/acetaminophen for pain, oral hydration. No dressing usually needed. Outpatient management.
    Partial-thickness (2nd°)Erythema + blisters (may burst/weep). Bleeds easily, very painful. Epidermis + outer dermis. Heals in ~3 wks. Scarring WILL occur.Do NOT break intact blisters (natural barrier). Gentle cleansing, topical antimicrobial (e.g., silver sulfadiazine per order), non-adherent dressing, pain control BEFORE dressing changes, monitor for infection.
    Full-thickness (3rd°)Variable color; dry, stiff, leathery; NO blanching; NO pain (nerve destruction). Needs excision + grafting.Airway/fluid priorities (see below), surgical referral for excision/grafting, strict infection prevention, monitor circulation if circumferential (compartment syndrome risk).
    Deep full-thickness (4th°)Extends into muscle/bone. No sensation. Amputation likely if digits affected.Same as 3rd° plus urgent surgical consult; monitor for rhabdomyolysis/myoglobinuria if muscle involved; assess neurovascular status frequently.
    🎯 High-yield: pain pattern is counterintuitive
    • MORE pain = shallower burn (1st/2nd° — nerves intact) · NO pain = deeper burn (3rd/4th° — nerves destroyed). A burn that doesn't hurt is not a mild burn.

    Differentiating Burn TYPES & Their Specific Interventions

    Extent — Total Body Surface Area (TBSA)

    • Adult "rule of nines" is INACCURATE in children — different body proportions (larger head, smaller legs)
    • Use age-specific charts (Lund and Browder) for pediatric TBSA estimation

    Three Phases of Burn Care

    PhaseFocus
    1. Burn shock / resuscitativeFirst 24–48 hrs; characterized by shock — airway & fluid resuscitation are priorities
    2. Recovery / wound healingClose the wound ASAP; infection prevention, nutrition, pain control
    3. RehabilitativePrevent scar contractures; reintegrate child into social environment

    TRUE Priority Order — What Kills First (ABC Framework)

    #1 PRIORITY — AIRWAY (kills within minutes)
    • Inhalation injury red flags: enclosed-space burn, singed nasal hairs/eyebrows, hoarse voice, soot on face, burned facial hair, burns to torso/face, swollen lips, hypoxia
    • SOB/dyspnea worse lying down, wheezing, gasping = acute pulmonary edema — EMERGENCY
    • Action: early intubation & ventilation; 100% oxygen
    • Notify provider immediately if: stridor, drooling, voice change, O2 sat dropping, retractions
    #2 PRIORITY — CIRCULATION / fluid resuscitation (kills within hours)
    • Third-spacing from ↑ capillary permeability, evaporative loss (no skin barrier), cell rupture → massive fluid needs
    • Monitor hourly urine output — the key indicator of resuscitation adequacy
    • Adequacy also judged by vital signs (especially HR), capillary refill, and sensorium/LOC — NOT skin turgor or edema level
    • Notify provider immediately if: UOP below target for 2 consecutive hours, HR rising with falling BP, altered LOC, cap refill >3 sec
    #3 PRIORITY — Compartment syndrome / circulation to extremities (kills the limb within hours)
    • Highest risk: circumferential 3rd-degree burns (arm/leg/torso)
    • Leathery, non-stretching skin + edema compresses nerves/vessels
    • Assess the 6 P's: Pain (out of proportion), Pallor, Pulselessness, Paresthesia, Paralysis, Poikilothermia (cool limb)
    • Action: elevate extremity, loosen constrictive dressings, notify provider — fasciotomy/escharotomy may be needed
    • Notify provider immediately if: any of the 6 P's develop, especially loss of pulse or worsening pain despite analgesia
    #4 PRIORITY — Infection / sepsis prevention (kills over days)
    • Immature immune system + open wound = high infection risk
    • Early sepsis signs: ↓LOC/lethargy, fever, restlessness, cap refill >5 sec — act on these, don't wait
    • Late sepsis sign: ↓BP — by this point the child is decompensating
    • Notify provider immediately if: new fever, increasing lethargy/irritability, wound odor/purulent drainage, rising WBC
    #5 PRIORITY — Nutrition, pain, and psychosocial (matters for recovery, not immediate survival)
    • NG to suction during burn shock phase; resume feeds after diuresis begins
    • ↑ protein/calorie needs during wound healing — see diet section below
    • Pain medication BEFORE dressing changes; diversional activities; encourage participation in care

    Labs & Diagnostics — Expected vs. Unexpected, with Interventions

    Lab/ParameterExpected in Burn Shock PhaseUnexpected / Concerning → Intervention
    Hematocrit (H&H)Initially ↑ (hemoconcentration from fluid shift into tissues)Persistently rising H&H despite fluids → notify provider, reassess fluid resuscitation rate
    Potassium (K⁺)May be mildly elevated early from cell lysis releasing intracellular K⁺Marked hyperkalemia (peaked T waves, arrhythmia) → notify provider immediately, cardiac monitoring
    Sodium (Na⁺)Hyponatremia common from fluid shifts/resuscitation fluidsSevere hyponatremia with seizures/lethargy → notify provider, may need fluid adjustment
    Albumin/total proteinDecreases from capillary leak of protein into tissuesVery low albumin with worsening edema → notify provider; may need colloid replacement
    GlucoseStress hyperglycemia is common and often expectedVery high or trending glucose with lethargy → notify provider, monitor for metabolic complications
    WBC countMild leukocytosis expected from the stress/inflammatory responseMarkedly rising WBC with fever/wound changes → notify provider, suspect infection
    Urine output (hourly)<30 kg: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr  |  >30 kg: 30 mL/hrBelow target x2 hrs → notify provider (under-resuscitation); myoglobinuria (tea-colored) in electrical/deep burns → notify provider, ↑ fluids to protect kidneys

    Dietary Needs During Wound Healing (Recovery Phase)

    Encourage — High Protein / High Calorie
    • Eggs, chicken, lean beef, fish, Greek yogurt, cheese, milk
    • Peanut butter, beans/legumes, protein shakes/supplements (e.g., PediaSure for younger children)
    • Calorie-dense additions: avocado, olive oil, nut butters, whole milk products
    • Vitamin C– and zinc-rich foods to support wound healing (citrus, berries, lean meats, nuts)
    • Frequent small meals/snacks — total caloric needs are significantly increased
    Limit — Low Nutritional Value
    • Empty-calorie snacks/sugary drinks that fill the child up without protein
    • Excessive plain water before meals in young children (can blunt appetite/intake)
    • NPO status during the acute burn shock phase per provider order — NG to suction until bowel sounds/diuresis return

    Contraindications & Restrictions

    ⚠ Restrictions During Care
    • Do NOT apply ice directly to a burn — causes vasoconstriction and can worsen tissue damage/hypothermia; use cool (not cold) running water only in the first few minutes
    • Do NOT break intact blisters — they are a natural infection barrier
    • Do NOT apply butter, oil, or home remedies to a burn
    • Grafted skin: NO sun exposure without protection — no functioning sweat glands, high overheating/burn risk
    • Activity restrictions per provider/PT during contracture-prevention splinting — do not skip prescribed positioning
    • Chemical burns: do NOT attempt to "neutralize" the chemical with another substance

    Self-Care Strategies (Recovery & Rehab Phase)

    • Perform prescribed range-of-motion exercises daily even when uncomfortable — prevents contractures
    • Wear compression/pressure garments as directed to minimize scarring
    • Moisturize healed/grafted skin regularly to reduce itching and dryness
    • Manage pruritus with prescribed antihistamines and by keeping skin cool rather than scratching
    • Protect grafted/healing skin from sun with clothing and provider-approved sunscreen once skin is healed enough
    • Attend all follow-up, PT, and OT appointments — missed sessions increase contracture risk

    Patient & Family Education

    Parent / Caregiver Education
    • Understand the extended timeline — healing, scarring, and rehab can take months
    • Learn dressing change technique and pain premedication timing for home care
    • Know the signs of infection or complications that require an immediate call to the provider
    • Arrange home health, PT/OT, and follow-up visits before discharge
    • Understand grafted skin has no sweat glands — supervise activity in heat and ensure hydration
    • Prepare for the child's emotional response to appearance changes; consider counseling resources
    Pediatric / Age-Appropriate Education
    • Toddler/preschool: Use simple, concrete words ("the doctor is making a hurt-spot boo-boo better"); allow choice when possible (which bandage sticker); use a comfort object during dressing changes
    • School-age: Explain what happened and why the skin needs special care in simple medical terms; let the child help with simple parts of care to build a sense of control
    • Adolescent: Address body image and scarring honestly; involve them in decisions about their care; connect with peer support if available; respect privacy during dressing changes
    • All ages: prepare the child before a dressing change ("first this, then this") rather than surprising them — reduces fear and improves cooperation
    🚩 Red flag: burns suggestive of abuse
    • Stocking/glove pattern — sharp, uniform immersion line = suspicious for deliberate dipping
    • Splash/splatter pattern with irregular borders = more consistent with accidental burn
